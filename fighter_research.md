# Fighter roster, achievement groups and research dossiers

Research date: 6 October 2026. Purpose: support authentic fighter animation, tactics and game content.

## Meaning of professionalism

All boxers listed here competed professionally. For this project, interpret the requested levels as demonstrated competitive achievement, assessed within the fighter's division and era. This is an editorial classification, not an official boxing ranking, character assessment or numerical measure of skill. Popularity, wealth and knockout percentage alone do not determine a group.

Use these criteria together: championship standing, quality of opposition, repeated success against elite opponents, duration of relevant success and historical recognition. Evaluate an era-specific version when implementing a fighter. Career achievement is metadata; it must not automatically multiply damage, stamina or reaction speed.

| Group | Meaning | Initial roster |
|---|---|---|
| A — Established legends | Sustained or historically significant championship achievements, elite opposition and substantial historical recognition | Muhammad Ali, Mike Tyson, Joe Frazier, George Foreman, Lennox Lewis, Evander Holyfield, Larry Holmes, Roberto Duran, Sugar Ray Leonard, Thomas Hearns, Marvin Hagler, Floyd Mayweather Jr., Bernard Hopkins |
| B — Accomplished world champions | Major world-title success and strong opposition; retained as a separate roster band for this project, with debatable boundaries | Riddick Bowe, James Toney, Mike McCallum, Ken Norton, Arturo Gatti, Diego Corrales |
| C — Leading world contenders | Demonstrated high-level opposition without a recognized major world championship | Jerry Quarry, Earnie Shavers |
| D — Developing/regional professionals | Early professional or regional achievement; requires a dated, separately researched roster | No named entries assigned in this edition |

Groups are not ordered within a row. Bowe, Toney and McCallum have credible cases for Group A; coach/historian review may move them. A Group C fighter can possess an exceptional individual weapon and defeat a higher-group fighter. Never identify amateur athletes as less professional simply because their competition uses different rules.

Katie Taylor, Amanda Serrano and Claressa Shields are included below as additional elite championship candidates. Their achievements merit inclusion in the main assessment process; their provisional separate review status reflects this edition's limited research, not a lower competitive level.

## Evidence boundaries

This edition contains sourced career research and structured tactical research briefs for 24 fighters. Style descriptions explicitly attributed to sources are documented foundations. Other mechanics, limitations and AI instructions are hypotheses to test against full bouts and coach review. Suggested footage is a study queue, not footage already watched or cleared for reuse. No full-bout annotation, mocap, independently measured biomechanics or completed deep film analysis is claimed.

Sources sometimes contain outdated records or internal errors. For example, Tyson's Hall of Fame record ends with his original retirement, and the Lewis biography incorrectly describes the Ruddock win as winning a vacant world title. Use the source for corroborated details, preserve disagreements, and verify exact title chronology against a second source before database ingestion. Avoid copying entire source records into game data.

## Group A dossiers

### Mike Tyson — proposed heavyweight version: 1986–1988

**Foundation:** Became heavyweight champion in 1986 and unified the WBC, WBA and IBF titles by 1987. The Hall of Fame specifically describes peek-a-boo defense, quick hands and swarming combinations. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/mike-tyson)

**Study focus:** Head movement coordinated with forward steps; compact guard; short-range hooks/uppercuts; body-to-head sequences; angle changes after entry; recovery when the opponent clinches or moves away. Establish which patterns recur rather than copying one highlight.

**AI hypothesis:** Seek safe entries into combination range, attack in bursts and reposition. Entry denial should force a new decision, not trigger teleportation or unlimited pursuit stamina.

**Footage queue:** Berbick, James Smith, Tony Tucker, Tubbs and Spinks; Douglas as a separate later-era comparison. Smith/Tucker provide longer fights that balance quick finishes. Preserve distinct era labels.

### Muhammad Ali — proposed heavyweight versions: 1964–1967 and 1974

**Foundation:** Three-time heavyweight champion. The Hall of Fame identifies speed, jab, low lead-hand positioning, pull-back defense and the Foreman rope-a-dope strategy. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/ali)

**Study focus:** Jab while moving, rhythm changes, distance-based defense and recovery; compare mobile early-career behavior with later rope tactics. Lower hands must leave plausible openings rather than confer automatic evasion.

**AI hypothesis:** Prefer space and jab-led exchanges in the early profile. Make rope tactics conditional in a separate later version, with actual defensive and stamina costs.

**Footage queue:** Liston, Cleveland Williams and Terrell for early-era study; Frazier, Norton and Foreman for separate later dossiers. Do not merge all bouts into a timeless prime version.

### Joe Frazier — proposed heavyweight version: 1968–1971

**Foundation:** Olympic gold medalist and undisputed heavyweight champion who defeated Ali in 1971. The Hall of Fame emphasizes his left hook and persistent attack. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/frazier)

**Study focus:** Crouching entries, head rhythm, pressure, body attack and hook setup; trace how foot position supports defense and offense.

**AI hypothesis:** Sustain positional pressure with a researched pace, rather than repeat a hook every tick. Test how reach and uppercut threats interrupt entry.

**Footage queue:** Bonavena, Ellis and Ali I; Foreman I as a later-era failure comparison. Defeat supplies evidence about conditions, not proof of a universal weakness.

### George Foreman — separate heavyweight versions: 1973–1974 and 1991–1994

**Foundation:** Heavyweight champion in two widely separated career periods; regained the championship against Moorer at age 45. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/foreman)

**Study focus:** Compare pressure, hand positioning, space control and attack pacing across the two versions. Investigate guard manipulation and balance from footage; the biography establishes career context rather than exact mechanics.

**AI hypothesis:** Author different movement and pacing policies for young and comeback Foreman. Power reputation does not justify guaranteed knockouts or immunity to fatigue.

**Footage queue:** Frazier, Norton and Ali for young Foreman; Holyfield, Morrison and Moorer for comeback Foreman.

### Lennox Lewis — proposed heavyweight version: 1999–2002

**Foundation:** Unified the major heavyweight titles after the Holyfield rematch and defeated Rahman in their rematch and Tyson. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/lennox-lewis)

**Study focus:** Long-range setup, jab/right-hand structure, range management, uppercuts and close-range control. Measure behavior by opponent type rather than treating height as tactical intelligence.

**AI hypothesis:** Maintain advantageous distance, commit when an opening appears and use supported close-range mechanics when crowded.

**Footage queue:** Holyfield I/II, Tua, Rahman I/II and Tyson. Earlier McCall material belongs to a different development period.

### Evander Holyfield — separate cruiserweight and heavyweight versions

**Foundation:** Unified cruiserweight champion and heavyweight champion with major wins against Douglas, Bowe and Tyson. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/evander-holyfield)

**Study focus:** Combination structure, exchange decisions, counters and changes with opponent size. Keep cruiserweight body/motion assets separate from heavyweight reference.

**AI hypothesis:** Permit both tactical boxing and committed exchanges; model visible recovery and fatigue rather than a limitless 'heart' statistic.

**Footage queue:** Qawi and DeLeon for cruiserweight; Bowe trilogy, Foreman, Tyson and Lewis for heavyweight versions.

### Larry Holmes — proposed heavyweight version: 1978–1982

**Foundation:** Long championship reign; the Hall of Fame explicitly identifies his signature left jab and documents title defenses. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/larry-holmes)

**Study focus:** Jab variations, movement, right-hand follow-ups, recovery after being hurt and control over long rounds.

**AI hypothesis:** Build around jab-led positioning and conditional follow-ups, preserving defensive openings and opponent counterplay.

**Footage queue:** Norton, Shavers, Weaver and Cooney. His later Tyson comeback is a separate version and should not set prime movement parameters.

### Roberto Duran — separate lightweight and 1980 welterweight versions

**Foundation:** Titles in four divisions; defeated Leonard for the welterweight title. The Hall of Fame describes relentless fighting. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/duran)

**Study focus:** Inside positioning, defense during attack, body work, feints and transitions between ranges. Establish hand-control details through annotation rather than assuming every close exchange is identical.

**AI hypothesis:** Pressure with opportunities to counter and defend. Missing inside-work/clinch systems would materially limit authenticity.

**Footage queue:** Buchanan and DeJesus for lightweight; Leonard I/II for welterweight; Moore and Barkley as separate later-weight cases.

### Sugar Ray Leonard — proposed welterweight version: 1979–1981

**Foundation:** Five-division title achievements; the Hall of Fame emphasizes adaptable strategy and finishing ability. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/ray-leonard)

**Study focus:** Movement-to-attack transitions, combination bursts and tactical changes. The Duran rematch and Hearns bout offer contrasting decision contexts.

**AI hypothesis:** Choose movement or sustained attack according to researched cues; never allow access to hidden future player input.

**Footage queue:** Benitez, Duran I/II and Hearns I; Hagler requires a separate later middleweight version.

### Thomas Hearns — separate welterweight and junior-middleweight versions

**Foundation:** Multi-division champion; the Hall of Fame specifically identifies his jab and right hand. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/thomas-hearns)

**Study focus:** Long-range threats, straight-punch setup, body work and behavior when crowded. Reach should derive from verified proportions, not invisible hitbox extension.

**AI hypothesis:** Favor effective long-range setups while allowing close exchanges where evidence supports them; defensive failure remains possible.

**Footage queue:** Cuevas and Leonard for welterweight; Benitez and Duran for junior middleweight; Hagler separately at middleweight.

### Marvin Hagler — proposed middleweight version: 1980–1987

**Foundation:** Middleweight champion with 12 successful title defenses, including Duran, Hearns and Mugabi. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/marvelous-marvin-hagler)

**Study focus:** Stance use, jab, pressure-to-counter transitions and pace changes. Verify stance-switch frequency and conditions from bouts before configuring it.

**AI hypothesis:** Support more than relentless attack: adjust pressure to opponent movement and available openings. Stance switching requires complete motion/contact support.

**Footage queue:** Minter, Duran, Hearns, Mugabi and Leonard. The short Hearns fight must not dominate estimates of ordinary pace.

### Floyd Mayweather Jr. — separate early and later professional versions

**Foundation:** The Hall of Fame's induction account identifies a five-division champion; trainer Calvin Ford discusses younger inside fighting versus later selective punching. [IBHOF induction](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/2022recap), [Ford interview](https://www.premierboxingchampions.com/news/fundamentals-calvin-ford)

**Study focus:** Defense selection, counter timing, distance and punch economy. Investigate shoulder-roll, high-guard and other defensive transitions through footage rather than force one guard at every range.

**AI hypothesis:** Make counters depend on observed cues and human reaction constraints. Defensive reputation must not become an automatic dodge probability.

**Footage queue:** Corrales and Castillo for early versions; De La Hoya, Cotto, Canelo and Pacquiao for later study. Verify exact version/weight separately.

### Bernard Hopkins — proposed middleweight version: 2001–2004

**Foundation:** Unified four major middleweight belts and held a long title reign; later won light-heavyweight titles. The Hall of Fame describes combination punching and defense. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/hopkins)

**Study focus:** Positioning, economical decisions, counters, inside exchanges and pacing. Separate older light-heavyweight adaptations from middleweight reference.

**AI hypothesis:** Reward useful positioning and legal close-range tactics; documented fouls must not silently become legal game actions.

**Footage queue:** Keith Holmes, Trinidad and De La Hoya; Taylor and Tarver for later comparison dossiers.

## Group B dossiers

### Riddick Bowe — proposed heavyweight version: 1992–1995

**Foundation:** Undisputed heavyweight champion; the Hall of Fame explicitly describes a long jab, power and infighting ability. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/bowe)

**Study focus:** Distinguish tall-fighter inside work from long-range play. **AI hypothesis:** allow effective attacks at multiple ranges without granting every tall fighter the same close-range skills. **Footage queue:** Holyfield trilogy, Dokes and Hide; Golota as a later comparison. Group A reassessment is appropriate after historical review.

### James Toney — separate middleweight/super-middleweight versions

**Foundation:** Multi-division world champion; documented counterpunching and shoulder-roll defense. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/copy-of-jones-roy)

**Study focus:** Defensive turns, counter opportunities, balance and inside exchanges. **AI hypothesis:** tie the roll to specific attack geometry, with failures and alternative guards. **Footage queue:** Nunn, McCallum and Barkley; Jones and Jirov as separate weight/era comparisons. Group A placement is debatable.

### Mike McCallum — separate junior-middleweight and middleweight versions

**Foundation:** Three-division world champion; the Hall of Fame identifies body punching and describes him as a boxer-puncher. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/mike-mccallum)

**Study focus:** Body-shot setups, counters and defensive recovery. **AI hypothesis:** increase body-attack preference when a visible opening exists, not regardless of distance/guard. **Footage queue:** Jackson and Curry; Kalambay and Toney for middleweight. Group A review remains open.

### Ken Norton — proposed heavyweight version: 1973–1978

**Foundation:** WBC heavyweight champion and an Ali rival; the Hall of Fame describes an awkward, powerful style. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/ken-norton)

**Study focus:** Guard configuration, footwork and how he disrupted Ali's rhythm. **AI hypothesis:** preserve specific positioning and openings rather than copy a generic pressure template. **Footage queue:** Ali trilogy, Foreman, Jimmy Young and Holmes.

### Arturo Gatti — separate 130-pound and 140-pound versions

**Foundation:** World titles at junior lightweight and super lightweight; the Hall of Fame documents his Ward trilogy and action-fighter reputation. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/arturo-gatti)

**Study focus:** Compare boxing periods with exchanges and comeback sequences. **AI hypothesis:** represent changes under different training/era contexts; exciting fights do not establish invulnerability. **Footage queue:** Rodriguez and Ruelas; Ward trilogy, Branco and Mayweather for later study.

### Diego Corrales — separate super-featherweight and lightweight versions

**Foundation:** World champion in both divisions; victories over Freitas and Castillo and losses to Mayweather/Casamayor provide contrasting contexts. [IBHOF profile](https://boxhall.wixsite.com/ibhof/corrales)

**Study focus:** Punching at close versus long distance, combination recovery and response to mobile opponents. **AI hypothesis:** verify actual range preferences rather than assume tall means outside boxer. **Footage queue:** Garcia, Mayweather, Casamayor, Freitas and Castillo I/II.

## Group C dossiers

### Jerry Quarry — proposed heavyweight version: late 1960s–early 1970s

**Foundation:** High-level contender who faced Ali, Frazier and Norton among other leading heavyweights. [BoxRec biography](https://boxrec.com/wiki/index.php/Jerry_Quarry)

**Study focus:** Counter opportunities, combinations and behavior against pressure versus power opponents. **AI hypothesis:** maintain a competitive toolset; contender classification is not beginner difficulty. **Footage queue:** Ellis, Frazier, Ali, Lyle and Shavers. Confirm results/dates through record sources before annotation.

### Earnie Shavers — proposed heavyweight version: 1973–1979

**Foundation:** Leading heavyweight challenger; documented bouts with Ali and Holmes, plus a knockout victory over Norton. [BoxRec biography](https://boxrec.com/wiki/index.php/Earnie_Shavers), [Norton–Shavers record](https://boxrec.com/wiki/index.php/Ken_Norton_vs._Earnie_Shavers)

**Study focus:** Punch setup, commitment, follow-through, defense and pacing. Test the power reputation against observable outcomes without treating anecdotal force comparisons as measurements. **AI hypothesis:** dangerous committed attacks with recoverable defensive openings. **Footage queue:** Ellis, Quarry, Ali, Norton and Holmes. A single weapon may be elite even without a world title.

### Katie Taylor

**Foundation:** Recognized in the 2022 WBO record as undisputed lightweight champion and joint female Fighter of the Year. **Study focus:** combination speed, feet during entry/exit and changes under pressure. **AI hypothesis:** conditional combination bursts with authentic recovery. **Footage queue:** Persoon, Serrano and Cameron series; verify each bout's weight/era. Proposed elite band pending the same historical review used for the men's roster.

### Amanda Serrano

**Foundation:** The same WBO record recognizes championship achievements in seven divisions. **Study focus:** stance, pressure, body attack and combination construction across selected weights. **AI hypothesis:** preserve version-specific proportions and range rather than combine seven divisions into one asset. **Footage queue:** Taylor, Erika Cruz and Heather Hardy; collect direct profile and bout sources before detailed annotation.

### Claressa Shields

**Foundation:** The WBO's 2022 recognition identifies two-division undisputed achievements following the Marshall victory. **Study focus:** jab/combination structure, defensive transitions and adaptation to different opponents. **AI hypothesis:** base adaptability on observed exchanges and bounded memory. **Footage queue:** Hammer, Marshall and Gabriels; choose one division/era first and verify records.

## Deep-research completion standard

For every implemented era, complete the following work, following Section 21 of fight_game.md:

1. Collect at least two independent career/reference sources and an authorized footage inventory. Verify identity, division, stance, physical references and relevant title chronology; flag conflicts.
2. Sample 6–10 complete bouts across opponent styles, competitive rounds and successes/failures where available. Short careers or scarce footage require an explicit limitation and adjusted sample.
3. Annotate timestamped exchanges, including setup, footwork, guard, punch sequence, result, recovery, ring position and opponent response. Record uncertain or occluded observations.
4. Have two analysts label a shared sample and a qualified coach resolve material disagreements. Publish agreement results, contextual frequencies and confidence; keep held-out bouts for validation.
5. Produce animation references and conditional Fighter DNA with traceable evidence. Validate normal-speed motion, slow-motion contact, AI style and player-controlled mechanics separately.
6. Review appearance/asset permissions independently of research quality. Mark each dossier's sources, film analysis, coach review, implementation, validation and rights clearance separately.

No fictional precision: do not assign unsupported punch-force values, reflex milliseconds, IQ, toughness, injury probabilities or '99 overall' ratings. Use provisional gameplay tuning labels until measurements/review justify tighter parameters. Preserve behavior-changing systems such as inside work, ropes and stance transitions as dependencies.

## Game organization

Store competitive group, division, era, style tags and evidence confidence separately. Show selectors for weight class and era, with optional achievement filters. Style tags can overlap: pressure, counterpunching, outside boxing, combination attack and inside work are useful dimensions rather than exclusive boxes.

Keep opponent difficulty and online skill rating separate from this roster classification and from coin stakes. A selectable easy Tyson may simplify decisions while preserving researched movement; it should not turn him into another fighter. Actual boxing matchups are not determined solely by achievement group.
